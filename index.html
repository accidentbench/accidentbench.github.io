ACCIDENT

A benchmark dataset for vehicle accident detection from traffic surveillance videos.

ACCIDENT is designed for evaluation in supervised, few-shot, and zero-shot settings. The benchmark combines 2,027 real clips and 2,211 synthetic clips, and focuses on temporal localization, spatial localization, and collision type classification in challenging CCTV footage.

Project overview Benchmarks
  • 2,027 real clips and 2,211 synthetic clips in the latest paper draft
  • Real-world CCTV footage with temporal, spatial, and type annotations
  • Synthetic data with richer annotations for transfer and data-scarce settings

Overview

A project website first, a leaderboard hub second

Why this benchmark

Most accident understanding work is built around dashcam data or narrow CCTV collections. ACCIDENT is structured around difficult surveillance footage and supports broader evaluation regimes, including supervised, zero-shot, and few-shot settings.

The website is drafted to present the paper clearly while also leaving room for multiple benchmarks, evaluation tracks, evolving code releases, and separate real-versus-synthetic benchmark views.

Core tasks

  • Temporal localization: when the accident happens.
  • Spatial localization: where the accident occurs in the frame.
  • Collision type classification: what type of event is observed.

Benchmark Design

Three benchmarks on a shared task definition

Benchmark 1

In-distribution

Acts as the standardized benchmark split, with train, validation, and test partitions constructed to preserve key attributes of the real dataset.

Benchmark 2

Geo-aware

Targets generalization under geographic shift by separating regions and testing whether methods transfer beyond the locations seen in training.

Benchmark 3

Zero-shot

Targets the setting in which no labeled training data is available and methods must operate without benchmark-specific supervision.

Drafting note: the benchmark always asks the same three questions, when, where, and what type of accident happened, while the public comparisons can be organized by these three benchmark splits.
